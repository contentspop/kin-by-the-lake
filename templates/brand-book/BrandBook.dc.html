A1 — Brand story

Two kitchens meet at the table

ForEveryone

Why it mattersIt gives the team and media one story to tell, the same way every time.
Use it whenWriting About Us, press releases, staff training, menu introductions.

The name

กิน (kin) is Thai for to eat. In English, kin means family. One word, two languages, the whole idea: eating together is what makes people family. Q1Who chose the name KIN, and is there a personal story behind it?Answer →

Why two kitchens

Phuket-Peranakan cooking is the island's own heritage: Hokkien, Malay and Thai, shaped over generations in old Phuket Town. Italian cooking is about simple produce treated with respect. Both are family food first. Q2Why Phuket-Peranakan and Italian? Is there a family, travel or chef story behind the choice?Answer →

Where they meet

Not in the pan. At the table. Both cultures already eat this way: the Peranakan tok panjang, a long table laid for the whole family, and the Italian Sunday lunch that runs into evening. Both carry the same blue too, Peranakan porcelain and Italian maiolica. So each dish stays true to its own kitchen, and they meet once they are served.

The place

A bamboo pavilion, a terrace and a dining hall beside Q3What is the lake called, and what is the exact location?Answer → in Phuket. The lake sets the pace: still water, long light, lanterns at dusk.
Phuket’s Peranakan world

Phuket’s Baba community grew from 19th-century Hokkien migration and the tin trade, and was tied to Penang by trade, schooling and family. Sailing to Penang was closer and safer than travelling to Bangkok.

Influence ran both ways: Phuket’s Vegetarian Festival (Nine Emperor Gods) travelled to Penang. Phuket belongs to the wider Straits Peranakan world of Penang, Malacca and Singapore.

Source: Khoo Salma Nasution, “Hokkien Chinese on the Phuket Mining Frontier”, JMBRAS 2009

In one line: KIN is where Phuket's own heritage cooking and Italy's family table, two cultures that already love a long shared meal, finally sit down together.

The context

Phuket has its own cuisine

Phuket is a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy (2015). Its Peranakan cooking, Hokkien, Malay and Thai shaped together in the old town, is living heritage, not a trend.

The gap

Good Peranakan food mostly lives in casual old-town shophouses. Good Italian mostly lives in resorts. Few places give both real attention in a setting made for long meals. Our reading of the market, to validate.

Proof · the plates met first
The plates crossed cultures before the food did.

Long before KIN, the ceramics of these cultures were already borrowing from each other. KIN is not inventing a fusion. It brings two kitchens that already met through porcelain back to one table.

Route 1

Chinese blue-and-white → Italian maiolica

Renaissance potters in Italy reinterpreted Ming blue-and-white. The vine pattern alla porcellana, “in the manner of porcelain”, was standard on maiolica by the 1550s.

Sources: Reeves Center, W&L · Britannica

Route 2

European enamel → Chinese famille rose

The pink of Nyonya ware began in Europe. Jesuit missionaries brought the gold-based pink enamel to China, where it was called yangcai, “foreign colours”. It was in use by the 1720s.

Sources: Spencer Museum of Art · V&A

Route 3

Nyonyaware, the Peranakan feast table

Porcelain made in China to Peranakan taste, with phoenix and peony in bright enamels, for the tok panjang feast table. A family service could run to hundreds of pieces.

Sources: Peranakan Museum · NHB Roots

Route 4

Majolica tiles in Peranakan homes

The tiles we call “Peranakan tiles” were imported majolica, from England, Belgium and Germany and later Japan, used from the mid-1800s to the 1950s. The tiles shown are English tiles of the period, not from a Peranakan home.

Sources: Peranakan Association Singapore · NHB Roots

All images © Victoria and Albert Museum, London. Internal reference only: ask the V&A for permission before any commercial use (ads, packaging, printed menus).

The one difference to decide

A Thai table puts every dish down at once. An Italian meal arrives in courses. KIN needs one clear rhythm, for example sharing dishes served in waves that follow an Italian course order. Q16How should dishes reach the table: all at once, Thai-style, or in waves like Italian courses?Answer →

Also from the research: sharing is central to Thai food culture (TAT, Suan Dusit University). Colatura di alici from Cetara is an anchovy sauce with roots in Roman garum (MICHELIN Guide), EU DOP since 2020 (Eunews). Phuket UNESCO City of Gastronomy, Mee Hokkien wheat noodles (PRD Thailand).

The insight

Two food cultures that look nothing alike on the plate behave exactly the same at the table. So KIN keeps the plates apart and brings the table together.

A6 — Dining approach

How we eat at KIN

ForEveryone
What this isHow a meal at KIN works, from ordering to the last plate.
Why it mattersThe format is the brand. If the experience feels like a normal restaurant, the story does not land.
Use it whenTraining staff, writing the menu, describing KIN to guests and media.

Two kitchens, two sides of the menu

Phuket-Peranakan on one side, Italia on the other. Each dish belongs to one kitchen and is never mixed on a plate.

Order to share

Order a few dishes, put them in the middle, pass them round, order a few more. Portions are made for sharing.

A table for the group

Round and long tables, so everyone can reach everything and nobody sits at the end.

Three times of day

Lunch · Sunset · Dinner. Each has its own rhythm. Q7KIN is open 11:00–23:00. Please confirm: brunch on which days, the last kitchen order, and does the full kitchen run through the afternoon?Answer →

Drinks that work with either kitchen, from aperitivo to Thai-botanical cocktails and zero-proof.

A dining space where timeless patterns meet the warmth of nature. Indigo botanical fabrics, rich wood grains, and softly glowing lanterns come together beneath a canopy of greenery. A9 — Core values

Simple. Natural. Timeless.

ForEveryone
What this isThe three values behind every decision, and the three things guests should leave with.
Why it mattersValues only work if they help decide things. Each one says what we do and what we refuse.
Use it whenMaking any choice: a dish, a photo, a partnership, a promotion.

Simple

Means: fewer things, done well.
Looks like: a short menu per kitchen, clean plates, calm design.
We don't: over-garnish, over-explain or over-decorate.

Natural

Means: real ingredients, real setting, real people.
Looks like: available light, the lake, honest service.
We don't: fake it with filters, props or staged smiles.

Timeless

Means: built to last, not to trend.
Looks like: heritage recipes, lasting materials, classic type.
We don't: chase trends that will look dated next year.

A10 — Personality & tone of voice

Speak like a host at the table

ForEveryone
What this isHow KIN sounds in every message, from menus to Instagram to LINE.
Why it mattersA consistent voice makes KIN recognisable even without the logo.
Use it whenWriting captions, menus, emails, signage, staff scripts and replies to reviews.

Generous

Warm, welcoming, inclusive. We invite, we don't sell.
"Pull up a chair. There's room."

Rooted

Precise about where things come from, proud of Phuket.
"Moo Hong, the way Phuket grandmothers make it."

Unhurried

Calm, considered, never pushy.
"Lunch that turns into sunset."
We sound like
  • Short sentences. One idea at a time
  • "We" and "you", like talking across the table
  • Real names of dishes, places and people
  • Thai written for Thai readers, not translated word for word
We never sound like
  • A brochure: "exquisite", "curated", "paradise"
  • Fusion talk: "East meets West", "a twist on"
  • Exclamation marks and hard selling
  • Two languages fighting in one headline
