SIMPLE. NATURAL. TIMELESS.

From the long lunch to the last lantern. The whole day KIN is built for: a lunch that runs long, the sunset, and dinner by lantern light, all beside still water.

Two kitchens, one table by the lake. The format. Two complete kitchens, each true to itself, both serving the same table.

Cooked apart, shared as KIN. The promise. We never blend the cuisines. We bring them together, and the sharing makes guests feel like kin.

A2 — Brand story

Two kitchens meet at the table

ForEveryone
What this isWhere the name comes from, why there are two cuisines, and how they belong together without becoming fusion.
Why it mattersIt gives the team and media one story to tell, the same way every time.
Use it whenWriting About Us, press releases, staff training, menu introductions.

The name

กิน (kin) is Thai for to eat. In English, kin means family. One word, two languages, the whole idea: eating together is what makes people family. Q1Who chose the name KIN, and is there a personal story behind it?Answer →

Why two kitchens

Phuket-Peranakan cooking is the island's own heritage: Hokkien, Malay and Thai, shaped over generations in old Phuket Town. Italian cooking is about simple produce treated with respect. Both are family food first. Q2Why Phuket-Peranakan and Italian? Is there a family, travel or chef story behind the choice?Answer →

Where they meet

Not in the pan. At the table. Both cultures already eat this way: the Peranakan tok panjang, a long table laid for the whole family, and the Italian Sunday lunch that runs into evening. Both carry the same blue too, Peranakan porcelain and Italian maiolica. So each dish stays true to its own kitchen, and they meet once they are served.

The place

A bamboo pavilion, a terrace and a dining hall beside Q3What is the lake called, and what is the exact location?Answer → in Phuket. The lake sets the pace: still water, long light, lanterns at dusk.

In one line: KIN is where Phuket's own heritage cooking and Italy's family table, two cultures that already love a long shared meal, finally sit down together.

00 — Principles

Eat as kin.

ForEveryone
What this isVoice rules, sample lines and the room's mood that the guidelines are built on.
Why it mattersIt is the bridge from the brand idea to the rules below.
Use it whenWriting anything, before opening the design files.

The brand idea. In Thai, กิน (kin) means to eat. In English, kin means family. KIN by the Lake is built on the space between the two words: the moment a shared table turns guests into kin.

Two kitchens, Phuket-Peranakan and Italian, each cooked with its own character and served to one round table. We never blend the cuisines. We bring them to the same table.

Personality

Generous
Plates that arrive for the middle of the table. Hospitality that notices.
ISAbundant, warm, inclusive NOTExcessive, loud, showy
Rooted
Precise about where every dish comes from, and proud of it.
ISHeritage-literate, authentic, specific NOTNostalgic kitsch, folkloric, "fusion"
Unhurried
The lake sets the pace. We give guests time back.
ISCalm, considered, lingering NOTSleepy, slow service, precious

Voice rules

  1. Speak like a host at the table, not a brochure. Use "we" and "you".
  2. Short sentences. Full stops, not exclamation marks. One idea per line.
  3. Name the ingredient, the place, the person. Leave out "exquisite", "curated", "fusion" and "paradise".
  4. Talk about time: long lunches, the light, the next course. Avoid words like "quick" and "hurry".
  5. Thai copy is written in Thai, never translated word-for-word. Keep it warm and polite (ค่ะ/ครับ only in direct service messages).
  6. Never use more than one language per headline. The second language goes underneath at a smaller size.

Italian & Peranakan words

  • Original names first, always. Mee Hokkien, Moo Hong, Tagliolini al Limone, then the description in English and Thai.
  • No italics for foreign words in running copy. Both cuisines belong here and are not set apart as exotic. (Italics above only mark examples.)
  • Keep accents (caffè, Nebbiolo, più). Romanise Phuket Hokkien the way it is spelled locally, then add Thai script: Moo Hong · หมูฮ้อง.
  • One cuisine per dish name. Never write "Tom Yum Risotto" or "Pad Thai Carbonara".
  • Italian words in social copy: no more than one per post (e.g. "Aperitivo by the lake"). Explain Peranakan words once, plainly.

A dining space where timeless patterns meet the warmth of nature. Indigo botanical fabrics, rich wood grains, and softly glowing lanterns come together beneath a canopy of greenery. Gentle curves and tactile materials create a sense of understated elegance, inviting guests to slow down, settle in, and enjoy the moment.

Translation into the system: indigo fabric becomes Porcelain Indigo, rattan becomes Bamboo Honey and lantern glow becomes Aged Brass. Wood grain and greenery appear in photography only and are never drawn as graphics.

Creative direction
Lanterns on the Water

Lake Blue and Warm Off-White carry the brand. Aged Brass is the glint of a lantern, so use it as a small accent. The reflection strokes are the signature. Porcelain pattern appears quietly and only as a supporting element. The room is already rich in pattern, so the graphics stay calmer than the room.

Guardrails
  • Blue-and-white must never read as Santorini, Greek or generic Mediterranean. Keep the white warm (#F3EFE6), never #FFFFFF.
  • No palm-leaf graphics, no extra wave or water icons, no kebaya-lace overload.
  • No red-checked cloths, no Italian flag colours, no visuals that mash the two cuisines together.
  • Lantern Red is a spark (≤3%). It should never read as Chinese New Year.

UNCOATED STOCK

541 U prints lighter and greyer. Specify 540 U to hold depth. Don't flood-print 7527: use a natural warm-white stock (e.g. Fedrigoni Arena Natural, Materica Gesso). Allow 10% dot gain and keep reversed Montserrat at ≥ 9 pt Regular.

SIGNAGE MATERIALS

Lake Blue powder-coat: drawdown-match to 541 C (closest stock is RAL 5001; approve a sample before production). Off-White: RAL 9001. Brass: marine-grade PVD brass on 316 stainless, satin #4 finish. Timber: thermally modified teak or ash, oiled.

BACKLIT & NIGHT

Light colour is lantern light: 2700–3000 K warm white only. Never use RGB or cool-blue LED. Lake Blue fields stay unlit; letters are halo-lit or have off-white acrylic faces. On screens at night, dim the off-white to 90% on Lake Blue.

BRASS: FOIL · STAMP · METAL

Foil: matt antique gold, matched to 871 C (e.g. Kurz Luxor matte gold or equivalent). Hot-stamp: minimum line 0.3 mm, minimum logo 25 mm, brass die for cloth, 0.2 mm deboss. Metal: brushed, lacquered or PVD. Raw brass only on hardware that is allowed to patina.

LOOPLESSButtons, nav, labels, wayfinding, the LINE rich menu and short social overlays (≤ 8 words).
  • Never letter-space Thai. Wide tracking is for Latin only.
  • Thai line-height ≥ 1.6 (display ≥ 1.35) so tone marks and lower vowels have room.
  • Thai has no capitals. Build hierarchy with size and weight only.

Secondary pattern · Porcelain Lattice

Four overlapping circles form a lobed quatrefoil. It draws on the central water feature, the encaustic floor tile and the porcelain rim, reduced to a single hairline. It is the only pattern in the system.

  • One colour, hairline. 12–18% opacity on screen, 20% tint in print.
  • Never more than 30% of a surface. Use it on borders, inner covers, packaging wraps, tissue and napkin bands.
  • Never behind the logo, text or photography. Never in brass or red.
  • The module is 24 mm in print and 56 px on screen. Never blow it up as a statement.
