01 · The idea

Golden hour to blue hour

Photography sells the experience more than any words. Every image should feel found, not staged, and warm, never cold.

Current images are renders from the design stage. Replace with KIN shoots when available.

Light

Shoot from 30 minutes before sunset to 30 after. Lanterns always lit. Available light plus one warm bounce, no flash. Keep the lake in every wide shot.

Food for sharing

Several plates in the middle, serving spoons in the dishes, plates part-eaten. House blue-and-white ceramics. One cuisine per frame.

People

Mid-conversation: hands reaching, a laugh caught side-on, mixed generations. No posing to camera, no clinking-glass clichés.

Grade

Warm highlights (3200 K feel), cool Lake Blue shadows, blacks lifted +4, saturation −8, greens toward olive, grain 2%. LUT: KIN_Lantern_v1.

Video

Locked-off or slow dolly only. 24 fps, 50% slow-mo for pours. Natural sound under one acoustic track. Cut on movement, hold each shot at least 2 seconds.

Proposal · Food photography

Food, as it arrives at the table

We suggest a light touch: real light, real service and real tables mid-meal, so the food looks as good as it does when it reaches the guest, not dressed up for the camera.

Proposal · for discussion
Natural light only

Daylight, golden hour and the real lanterns. No flash, no studio lights.

Food as it is served

The plate as it leaves the kitchen. No tweezers, no oil brushed on for shine, no garnish that is not in the recipe.

Props from KIN

KIN’s blue-and-white plates, linen, glasses and cutlery. Nothing brought in to fill the frame.

Signs of a meal

Crumbs, a sauce already shared, a half-finished glass, a used napkin.

Real people, real moments

Hands passing, serving, pouring. Nobody posing for the camera.

A light edit

Food colour true to life, warmth that matches the real light, no pushed saturation.

A

Lunch to Last Light

Suggested lead direction

The same table through the day, photographed as it happens: daylight through the umbrellas at lunch, the lake behind the glasses at golden hour, then lantern light only. The photographer sits in the moment rather than arranging it.

How to shoot

Documentary. 45° at table height and wide frames with the lake in view. Shoot the same table at three times of day. Available light, one warm bounce at most.

Where it is used

Website hero, awareness campaigns, the “a day at KIN” story, press.

B

Blue on the Table

Overhead, during a real meal. KIN’s blue-and-white plates with dishes from both kitchens on the same table, some already half-eaten, hands reaching in. The table is not lined up; it is a table being enjoyed.

How to shoot

Directly overhead or a high 70°. Soft daylight from one side. Linen in Warm Off-White, real cutlery, no extra props. One cuisine per plate, both on the table.

Where it is used

Menu pages, website menu, social grid, delivery and booking platforms.

C

The Host’s Hands

Close and quiet: a plate carried out of the kitchen, sauce poured at the table, cheese grated, wine poured. Light from the window or a lantern, the real room behind, never a studio backdrop.

How to shoot

Close, 50–85 mm, shallow depth. Shoot during service, not staged. Hands and aprons, faces rarely. Low light is fine; keep the grain.

Where it is used

Social stories and reels, team and hospitality stories, menu inserts.

C · in practice · one dish, four moments

Follow one dish through its life at the table instead of shooting it once: served, the wine poured, finished at the table, enjoyed. Same table, same light, a few minutes apart. It gives a natural carousel or a short reel without any extra styling.

D

The Plate, Simply

One plate, the food as the subject. Nothing added around it: the dish as the kitchen sends it out, a plain ground, and one soft directional light so the textures read. Simple, close and honest, the way a guest sees the plate when it lands.

How to shoot

Overhead or 45°, the plate filling most of the frame. Natural window light from one side, or a single shaft of light. Grounds from KIN itself: the table wood, off-white linen, stone or the Lake Blue banquette. No garnish that is not in the recipe.

Where it is used

Menu and website menu, signature dish posts, delivery and booking platforms, press kit.

02 · Rules

Do and don’t

Do
  • Show the pavilion reflected in still water at dusk
  • Include a lit oil lantern on every table shot
  • Shoot hands sharing from the middle of the table
  • Use toile and tile as soft, out-of-focus context
  • Leave calm space (sky, water) for type
Don’t
  • Harsh midday sun, flash or cool-white LED
  • Garnish, props or backdrops brought in only for the photo
  • Thai and Italian dishes “fused” in one styled shot
  • Teal-orange grading or HDR skies
  • Palm-frame postcard shots or a lantern-red flood
